# Falsifiable predictions and theorem links

1. With equal initial TPR, a difference in effective correction produces the
   exact final gap ((1-t)|\kappa_A^+-\kappa_B^+|) (Theorems 2 and 5; EXP-01).
2. Raising reviewer accuracy equally can enlarge the absolute fairness gap
   when appeal propensities differ, because it scales their difference
   (Theorem 7; EXP-02/03).
3. Equal assistance can preserve, widen, or narrow effective-access disparity
   depending on operative CDF levels and densities; stochastic dominance signs
   the level but not the change in magnitude (Theorem 7 and the rejected
   monotonicity conjecture; EXP-03).
4. A targeted allocation can meet a common residual-risk target at the exact
   threshold budget in Proposition 6, while a uniform allocation can fail at
   the same budget (EXP-04/05).
5. Two systems with indistinguishable appeal logs can have different latent
   final FNR disparity (Theorem 13; EXP-06).
6. Representative random audits make latent residual error identifiable, and
   interval width should contract at the (1/\sqrt{rN}) scale away from
   boundary instability (Theorem 15 and Proposition 8; EXP-08).

These are predictions to test, not empirical findings.
