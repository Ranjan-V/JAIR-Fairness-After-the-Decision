# Relation to adjacent problems

## Algorithmic recourse

Recourse typically asks which changes to features or actions can produce a
different future model decision.  Contestation here concerns correction of an
already-issued decision through institutional review.  The action changes
access to a review channel, not necessarily the person's underlying features.

## Learning to defer

Deferral assigns a case to AI or human before or during prediction.  Here the
AI has already decided, and the affected person endogenously selects into a
later correction stage.  The missing counterfactual concerns non-appellants.

## Selective labels

Selective-label work studies labels missing because earlier decisions control
which outcomes become observable.  Here missingness is generated by selective
entry into a post-decision appeal mechanism.  The mathematics overlaps
missing-data theory, but Theorem 13 fixes the selection mechanism and target to
post-contestation fairness.

## Procedural fairness

Procedural fairness is broader than this model.  This project distinguishes a
formally symmetric input (the same subsidy or nominal procedure) from equality
of the effective transition probability induced by heterogeneous costs.

## Auditing

Audits are institution-initiated sampling; appeals are individual-initiated.
Theorem 15 uses random audits as a design intervention that repairs the
selection problem created by appeals.

## Workload-constrained review

Budgeted review normally chooses which cases to send to a human.  Here people
first decide whether to seek correction, and policy changes the distribution
of that entry.  The discrete allocation limit contains knapsack, but that
generic complexity fact is supporting rather than the conceptual novelty.
