# Research question

This project studies a two-stage sociotechnical decision rule.  An AI issues
an initial decision (D_0); an affected person may selectively invoke a human
correction channel; the institution ultimately acts on (D_1).  The central
question is not whether (D_0) is fair in isolation, but when a fairness
constraint satisfied by (D_0) is preserved, destroyed, or unidentifiable for
(D_1).

The mathematical object is the group- and label-specific stochastic
transition from (D_0) to (D_1).  In the adverse-only model its two effective
parameters are (\kappa_g^+=\alpha_g^+\rho_g^+), correction of a false denial,
and (\kappa_g^-=\alpha_g^-\rho_g^-), false reversal of a correct denial.
Self-selection matters because (\alpha) depends on private costs, beliefs,
and assistance, while ordinary appeal logs omit ground truth for people who do
not enter review.

The research questions are:

1. Which fairness constraints are invariant under contestation operators?
2. When does equal formal access fail to produce equal effective correction?
3. What final-error and fairness quantities are identified from appeal logs?
4. How should limited assistance be allocated under explicit fairness and
   welfare objectives?

All claims are associational unless an intervention or random-audit design is
explicitly stated.
