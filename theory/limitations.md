# Limitations

The binary one-shot model omits ranking, multiclass decisions, repeated appeals,
queues, deadlines, learning, and retaliation.  A group-level threshold response
can hide within-group inequity and behavioral heterogeneity.  Perceived benefit
and success may themselves respond to assistance, contradicting a pure cost
shift.  Reviewer accuracy may depend on case mix, so increasing appeals can
change (\rho).

Ground truth may be contested, delayed, or affected by the decision.  Random
audits identify label prevalence only if selection is representative and audit
labels are valid.  Protected-group categories can be incomplete or legally
restricted.  Fairness constraints do not settle normative questions about
which errors matter or how welfare should be aggregated.

The operator theorem is general linear algebra; the contribution depends on
the contestation-specific specialization, endogenous selection, and
identification results.  The impossibility theorem is conditional and has
clear escape routes.  The discrete NP-hardness reduction is standard and
should not be marketed as a headline novelty by itself.

No literature-priority claim is made.  A reliable source-based literature
review is still required before any publication claim.
